** The SEBD Grand Tour 🍾
The SEBD grand tour, 1993–2026 — with provenance and a certificate. From Bertram
Ludäscher & two AI toolchains, the morning of the SEBD'26 closing talk.

[badge:AI prose] Thirty-four editions ago, SEBD — founded by
[[https://dblp.org/pid/s/DomenicoSacca.html][Domenico Saccà]] — began in Gizzeria Lido;
this year it reached Senigallia, where the closing talk was about /trustworthy AI
results/: answers that arrive with evidence, not just confidence. The morning of the
talk, as a small self-referential demo, we asked: /what is the shortest grand tour
visiting every SEBD edition, from the first to the thirty-fourth?/ This page is the
keepsake version of that demo — and, in the spirit of the talk, it does not ask you to
believe it. Every number below carries the means to check it. Let it make you smile —
and let you verify.

[badge:primary source] The 34 editions and their host cities come from the
[[https://dblp.org/db/conf/sebd/index.html][DBLP index of SEBD proceedings]] (2026
Senigallia supplied by hand — DBLP doesn't have the future yet). Their coordinates come
from [[https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/][OpenStreetMap/Nominatim]], checked place by
place on 2026-08-01 — see [[#coords][the coordinate audit]] for why that sentence used
to read differently, and what it cost.

The page has two parts. [[#part1][Part I]] is the /provenance/: how the answer came

coordinates. [[#part2][Part II]] is the /evidence/: a tree of what the final claim

The errors are not random, which is the interesting part. All four stops are coastal
/frazioni/ — a Lido, a Marina, a beach hamlet — and in every case the coordinate had
drifted inland toward the parent comune that shares the name. That is exactly the
failure mode you would predict of a language model recalling a place from its name
rather than looking it up, and exactly what a gazetteer catches in one pass. The other
30 stops came back within 1.5 km. [badge:primary source]

What it cost: the certified optimum was *3,541 km* and is now *3,527 km*; the
chronological comparison moved 15,245 → 15,209 km. What it did /not/ cost: the visiting
order is unchanged, and every structural claim on this page — the sandwich argument, the
six cuts, the 16 co-optima — survived verbatim. A certificate proves the tour is
shortest /for the numbers it was given/. It has nothing whatever to say about whether
those numbers describe Italy. That is not a flaw in the method; it is the precise
boundary of what the method claims, and this page spent two weeks demonstrating it by
accident.

The GPT bundle, for its part, had Torre Canne and Squillace Lido right all along, and
Castellaneta Marina wrong by 10.0 km and Gizzeria Lido by 3.5 km — so neither toolchain
was trustworthy on coordinates, and neither was checkable without a third source.
[badge:verified]

* An unresolved fact — where was SEBD 1998?
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: venue1998
:END:
The edition table above, like both AI toolchains, follows DBLP: the 6th SEBD (1998) was
in *Ancona*. On 2026-10-20 a third source turned up that disagrees. The symposium's own
[[https://sebd.inf.uniroma3.it/history.html][history page]], written by a participant,
says: “Nel 1998 siamo tornati al mare, a Portonovo, un posto di sogno (scogli e
spiaggia) vicino ad Ancona” — /in 1998 we went back to the sea, to Portonovo, a dream
spot (rocks and beach) near Ancona/. Portonovo also hosted the 14th SEBD in 2006. If
1998 was there too, editions 6 and 14 are a fourth twin pair, and the tour's input
changes. [badge:primary source]

Searching further did not settle it. The proceedings title says “Ancona”; the official
[[https://sebd.inf.uniroma3.it/index.html#events][events list]] says “Ancona, Marche”
but links each edition to DBLP, so it repeats the same witness; papers cite the
proceedings, not the venue; and an AI assistant (Gemini) offered that the meeting was
“highly likely” on the Ancona engineering campus, without a source. Portonovo lies
within the comune of Ancona, so “Ancona” may name the place at a coarser resolution.

Rather than pick a winner, we wrote the situation down as rules
([[file:evidence/venue-1998/venue1998.lp][venue1998.lp]]), keeping three kinds of
statement apart:

- *Known* (strict facts): what each source says; that the events list repeats DBLP and
  that the Gemini claim cites no source (so both are /undercut/); that Portonovo lies
  within the comune of Ancona.
- *Assumed* (an explicit choice, both options left open): what “Ancona” on the title page
  refers to — the city where the meeting was held, as proceedings titles usually do, or
  only the comune, which includes Portonovo.
- *Defeasible*: a reliable source saying X supports X, unless the conflicting venue holds.

An answer-set solver then reports every reading the evidence allows, and which
assumption each one makes. The diagram is drawn /from/ the rules, node by rule; it is a
view of them, not a translation into an argumentation framework, which would lose the
structure that says /why/ each argument stands or falls.

# --- div.legend ---
*How to read the runs, without answer-set programming.*

*An answer set is a consistent story.* The solver lists every story that respects the
rules. A story must contain all the known facts; pick one meaning of “Ancona”; conclude
a venue only if a reliable source supports it and nothing in the same story defeats it;
and contain nothing it cannot justify from the rules.

*Why three stories.* If “Ancona” names only the comune, the label says nothing about the
city, so only the eyewitness speaks: Portonovo — one story. If it names the city, the
eyewitness and the label each support their venue “unless the other venue holds”.
Neither rule outranks the other, so both choices are consistent: one story where the
eyewitness prevails (Portonovo), one where the label does (Ancona city).

*Reading a line.* Each word is one fact of that story: =witness(history)= — the history
page counts as a witness; =undercut(events)= — the events list is discounted;
=label_means(city)= — this story assumes “Ancona” names the city; =venue(portonovo)= —
this story's answer; =at(ancona_municipality)= — the meeting was in the comune.

*Cautious and brave.* Cautious lists what /every/ story agrees on: what the evidence
establishes. Brave lists what /at least one/ story contains: what remains possible.

*The what-ifs.* Adding a fact removes stories. The question is settled when every
remaining story has the same venue.

What holds in /all/ of them is what we know: the meeting was within the comune of
Ancona, and the events list and the campus claim add no independent weight. What we do
not know is the venue. Portonovo is possible under either assumption; Ancona city is
possible only if the label names the city /and/ is preferred over the eyewitness. That
asymmetry is the honest summary — the evidence leans toward Portonovo — but it is not a
proof, and the page keeps both. [badge:verified] (clingo 5.6.2, 2026-10-20; outputs in
the [[file:evidence/venue-1998/README.org][README]])

The rules also say what would settle it. Testimony from a 1998 organizer for either
venue leaves one answer in every reading — a question for Paolo Atzeni, Luca Cabibbo or
Maurizio Panti. Fixing the assumption does less: reading the label as the comune leaves
only Portonovo, but reading it as the city still leaves the question open, because the
eyewitness disagrees. /Corrected before publication:/ a first version of these rules
stated as a known fact that “Ancona” names the comune, while also letting it support the
city; a review note caught the hidden assumption, which is now explicit.

For the tour, nothing has changed yet: it keeps 1998 at Ancona's coordinates. Under the
Portonovo reading, edition 6 would move about 9 km and become a twin of edition 14, and
the optimum would have to be re-solved and re-certified. That has not been done.

* Why believe "optimal" without trying every tour?
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sandwich
:END:
A fair objection: nobody enumerated the alternatives. With endpoints fixed, the 32
intermediate editions can be ordered in 32! ≈ 2.6 × 10^{35} ways. At a billion tours
checked per second, exhaustive search would take about 8 × 10^{18} years — some 600
million times the age of the universe. Exhaustion is not merely slow; it is not a thing
that happens. [badge:verified] (arithmetic you can redo on a napkin)

The proof works differently — it is a /sandwich/, not a search. Two independent facts
are produced:

- *An upper bound:* a concrete valid tour of length 3,527 km exists (check it: sum its
  legs — =verify.pl= does exactly this).
- *A lower bound:* /no/ tour — seen or unseen — can be shorter than 3,527 km.

The lower bound comes from /relaxation/: drop the requirement "the edges form one
connected tour" and demand only "every stop touches exactly 2 edges." (A path has two
endpoints of degree 1, so =exact_tsp.py= first closes ours into a cycle with a zero-cost
dummy stop joined to editions 1 and 34 — degree 2 then forces both free edges in, and
the cheapest cycle is exactly the cheapest path.) Every real tour satisfies these weaker
constraints, so the minimum over this larger set can only be ≤ the best tour — a valid
bound. The first relaxation yields 2,881 km, but its "solution" is six disconnected
mini-loops. So a /subtour-elimination cut/ is added — a linear inequality saying "at
least 2 edges must cross this mini-loop's boundary," which is self-evidently true of
every genuine tour, so the bound stays valid — and the relaxation is re-solved. Six
iterations (2881 → 3273 → 3439 → 3475 → 3477 → 3527) replace 10^{35} evaluations; at
3,527 the relaxation's own solution /is/ a single tour, so lower = upper = optimum. This
is the Dantzig–Fulkerson–Johnson method (1954), the engine inside the record-holding
Concorde TSP solver. [badge:verified]

And a footnote the proof itself supplies: the optimal /value/ is unique, the optimal
/tour/ is not. An enumeration run on 2026-08-01 re-solved with a /no-good cut/
forbidding each optimum already found, until the solver's best answer exceeded 3,527 (it
did so at 3,529). It reported *16 optimal tours*: two routes, each written 8 ways. Note
the status of that count: it was /reported/, but the enumeration script is not in the
published bundle and nothing here certifies it. The certificate proves the optimal
/value/, not the number of tours that attain it.

** Two kinds of “the same tour”
The 16 are not 16 of one kind. They are 2 × 8, and the two factors mean quite different
things.

*The ×8 is a symmetry of the data.* Rimini (editions 2 and 18), Venice (9, 20), and
Villasimius (28, 32) each hosted twice, at identical coordinates, so each pair is 0 km
apart and the best tour visits them back to back. Writing Venice as “9, 20” or “20, 9”
is the same trip with the labels swapped; three pairs give 2³ = 8 ways to write any
tour. This holds under any metric, any rounding, any coordinates: it comes from our
choice to key the tour by /edition/ rather than by city, and it is checkable in one line
per pair (=distance(2,18,0)=, =distance(9,20,0)=, =distance(28,32,0)= in
[[file:evidence/Claude-artifacts/distance.pl][distance.pl]]).

*The ×2 is a coincidence of rounding.* The second route differs only at the very end,
visiting Portonovo before Ancona instead of after. Ancona and Portonovo are 9 km apart,
and with distances rounded to whole kilometers the two endings tie exactly. Without the
rounding they do not:

# TABLE -- one bullet per row; the sub-bullets are the columns.
- Rimini → Ancona → Portonovo → Senigallia (published)
  - /Rounded legs (km):/ 93 + 9 + 35
  - /Rounded total:/ 137
  - /Unrounded total:/ 136.16 km
- Rimini → Portonovo → Ancona → Senigallia
  - /Rounded legs (km):/ 101 + 9 + 27
  - /Rounded total:/ 137
  - /Unrounded total:/ 136.54 km

So in the unrounded geometry there is one optimal route, written 8 ways; the second
route ties only in the rounded model, which is the model the certificate is about. Nudge
a coordinate or round differently and the tie breaks. [badge:verified] (the arithmetic,
2026-10-20, from the coordinates in [[file:evidence/Claude-artifacts/sebd.pl][sebd.pl]]
and the projection in [[file:evidence/Claude-artifacts/trip.py][trip.py]])

What the evidence covers, and what it does not: the certificate proves that no tour is
shorter than 3,527 km, and =verify.pl= checks one tour of exactly that length. That each
of the 16 is 3,527 km, and that the twin swaps never change a length, would be easy to
add as checks; nobody has. That there are /no other/ optimal tours would need a
different certificate — the same kind of case tree, but proving that every tour outside
the 16 is at least 3,528 km. /Added 2026-10-20:/ until then this page described both
factors in one breath, as “two genuinely different routes, each in 8 twin-relabellings”,
and said the detour “cancels exactly” without saying that only the rounding makes it
cancel.

Which is why, if you run =exact_tsp.py= yourself, its last line prints a visiting order
that is /not/ the one published above: it is the other route, the one that ends
Portonovo → Ancona. Both are among the 16. The solver reports whichever optimum it
reaches first; the route file records the one the heuristic found. Nothing is wrong —
but it is exactly the kind of discrepancy that should be stated in advance rather than
discovered by a suspicious reader.

    [[file:evidence/Claude-artifacts/exact_tsp.py][exact_tsp.py]] — but you do need (a)

    node), (b) that each subtour cut is a valid inequality, and (c) that HiGHS correctly

That is the asymmetry the talk turns on: the /solver/ may be a thousand lines of
heuristics and floating point — irrelevant, because what it emits is a witness whose
checking burden is tiny. How the tour was /found/ (2-opt? simulated annealing? divine
inspiration?) never enters the argument. Trust the certificate, not the solver.

* A proof with no solver in it
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: nosolver
:END:
"Trust the certificate, not the solver" was, until 2026-08-04, an /aspiration/ on this
page: the tour was checkable by hand, but /optimality/ still went through HiGHS. That
residual is now closed. In [[file:evidence/certificate/][evidence/certificate/]] there
is a proof object that needs no solver, no linear algebra, no floating point — only
integer arithmetic:

# VERBATIM -- transcript of an actual run; do not edit the block below.
#+begin_example
cd evidence/certificate
swipl -q -g run -t halt check_certificate.pl
#+end_example

Actual output on 2026-08-04 (macOS, SWI-Prolog 9.0.4, 0.6 s):

# VERBATIM -- transcript of an actual run; do not edit the block below.
#+begin_example
PASS (1) 563 edges = 561 published distances + 2 dummy edges
PASS (2) node 35 joins only stops 1 and 34, at zero cost
PASS (3) all 34 cuts are proper subsets (2 =< |S| =< 33)
PASS (4) the 226 leaves form a complete binary tree -- exhaustive
PASS (5) all 226 leaves bound every tour above 3526 km, so >= 3527 km
PASS (6) the published route is a valid 1..34 tour of 3527 km

VERIFIED: no tour of the 34 SEBD editions is shorter than 3527 km,
and a tour of exactly 3527 km exists.  3527 km is optimal.
#+end_example

The whole thing rests on one inequality. Give every stop a number /y_{v}/ (any numbers)
and every subtour cut a weight /z_{S}/ ≥ 0 (any weights), and give each edge a /reduced
cost/

# VERBATIM -- transcript of an actual run; do not edit the block below.
#+begin_example
d_e  =  c_e - y_u - y_v - (sum of z_S over the cuts that edge e crosses)
#+end_example

Then /every/ tour costs at least

# VERBATIM -- transcript of an actual run; do not edit the block below.
#+begin_example
2*(sum of all y_v)  +  2*(sum of all z_S)  +  (sum of min(0, d_e) over all edges)
#+end_example

Why: every stop has exactly two tour edges, every tour crosses every cut at least twice,
and a 0/1 variable can only hurt you where /d_{e}/ is negative. Three lines of algebra —
and, crucially, /no assumption about where the numbers came from/. A certificate that
would break if its numbers came from a liar is not a certificate. [badge:verified] (the
inequality) — but see [[#second-opinion][a second opinion]]: the /checker/ did not
enforce the inequality's preconditions until 2026-10-16.

One such inequality is not enough, and the shortfall is worth knowing: the best bound
any single page of potentials can give — 35 numbers and 7 cuts, the full Held–Karp
relaxation — is *3,447 km*, some 80 km short. So the proof becomes a case analysis: a
binary tree splitting on "this edge is used / is not used," where each branch is closed
by its own inequality. Ours has *226 leaves*, maximum depth 16, a pool of 34 cuts, about
16,000 integers, 99 KB. [badge:verified]

That size /is/ the finding, and it sharpens the talk's thesis rather than softening it:
a napkin proves the tour exists; a small book proves that nothing beats it; and checking
the small book still takes 0.6 seconds against 8 × 10^{18} years of exhaustive search.
Note what check (4) does — it verifies the case analysis has no /hole/. A missing leaf
is not an error in a proof; it is an unexamined case, which is exactly the failure a
plausible-looking certificate would hide.

And to answer the obvious suspicion — does the checker actually bite, or does it pass
everything? Six tampered copies were run against it:

# TABLE -- one bullet per row; the sub-bullets are the columns.
- delete one leaf
  - /Response:/ =FAIL (4) the case analysis has a hole=
- zero out one leaf's potentials
  - /Response:/ =FAIL (5) a leaf does not close=
- make a cut multiplier negative
  - /Response:/ =FAIL (5) a leaf does not close=
- claim 3,528 km instead
  - /Response:/ =FAIL (5) a leaf does not close=
- falsify one edge cost (645 → 545 km)
  - /Response:/ =FAIL (1) an edge disagrees with distance.pl=
- enlarge a cut to the whole node set
  - /Response:/ =FAIL (3) a cut set is not a proper subset=

One tampering is /accepted/, and should be: nudge a potential by a few units and you
still have a valid proof, just a different one. A checker that rejected that would be
policing provenance, not validity. [badge:verified] (all seven runs; see
[[file:evidence/certificate/README.org][README.org]])

What is still trusted, stated plainly: SWI-Prolog's integer arithmetic; the checker
itself (short enough to read, but written by the same agent that wrote the generator —
and, as the next section shows, short enough to read is not the same as correct); and,
as ever, the 561 distances, which are a claim about a model, not about Italy.

* A second opinion — the checker had a hole
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: second-opinion
:END:
On 2026-10-16 another AI agent, OpenAI Codex, reviewed the abstract and this page
without being told what we already knew. It read the checker, found what it does not
check, and built a forgery that exploits it: give every leaf of the certificate a 36th
“potential” for a node that does not exist, and claim the /chronological/ route of
15,209 km. The bound counts the extra number; no edge ever subtracts it. The 2026-08-04
checker printed: [badge:verified] (reproduced here)

# VERBATIM -- transcript of an actual run; do not edit the block below.
#+begin_example
VERIFIED: no tour of the 34 SEBD editions is shorter than 15209 km,
and a tour of exactly 15209 km exists.  15209 km is optimal.
#+end_example

The inequality was never wrong; the checker failed to enforce its preconditions (exactly
35 potentials, a model fixed in advance) and loaded the certificate as program code
rather than data. =verify.pl= had a smaller gap of the same kind: it summed the stored
legs without checking that they follow the visiting order. Six well-chosen tamperings,
all rejected, had not been enough to show that the checker bites — a familiar lesson
about tests written by the author of the code.

What survived, and why we know: *the 3,527 km result stands.* The unchanged certificate
passes the hardened checker, and also a second checker Codex wrote for the review —
Python, standard library, integer arithmetic, a different author in a different
language, now shipped as
[[file:evidence/certificate/independent_check.py][independent_check.py]]. That is
exactly the step the [[#gaps][gaps ledger]] had listed as “the next honest step” since
August. [badge:verified]

What changed (2026-10-16): the checker now reads the certificate as data, fixes the
model's size itself, and runs a new check (0) on the shape of every fact before anything
else; =verify.pl= computes the length from the visiting order and checks each stored leg
against it. The forgery and four related ones are now regression tests that must fail
([[file:evidence/certificate/tests/run_tests.py][tests/run_tests.py]]; run with
=--original= to watch the August checker accept three of them). Every changed file, with
hashes before and after, is logged in
[[file:evidence/certificate/CORRECTIONS.org][certificate/CORRECTIONS.org]] and
[[file:evidence/Claude-artifacts/CORRECTIONS.org][Claude-artifacts/CORRECTIONS.org]];
the originals are kept beside them.

The same review also caught four errors in this page's prose: a table that gave the
post-audit length as if it had been reported on the morning of the talk, a sentence that
had the direction of the coordinate error backwards, a spreadsheet cross-check pointing
at the other toolchain's distance table, and an overstated rounding bound. All four are
corrected above and marked where they were. And it qualified one claim rather than
correcting it: the count of 16 co-optimal tours is reported, not certified.

So the second opinion did what the page says second witnesses are for. The first time
(the coordinates, 2026-08-01) a disagreeing source caught bad /data/; this time a
disagreeing reader caught a bad /checker/. In both cases the certified claim survived,
and in both cases only because someone other than its author looked.

* Appendix: a side experiment from the ASP world
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: asp
:END:
One cross-check deserves a footnote: a generic clingo (answer-set programming) encoding
of the tour problem ([[file:evidence/Claude-artifacts/tsp.lp][tsp.lp]]) reached only
6,709 km after 90 seconds — corroborating, never competing. That feebleness is a known
/encoding/ issue, not an ASP one: Bertagnon & Gavanelli (Univ. Ferrara) give new TSP
encodings in clingo[DL] — constraint ASP over difference logic — that are orders of
magnitude faster, with a further geometric speedup for exactly our case, Euclidean
instances with known coordinates ([[https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/exaf072][/J. Logic
and Computation/ 36(2), 2026, open access]]). [badge:primary source] (the paper's
claim).

In this page's spirit, we ran the experiment rather than just citing it (2026-07-18,
same laptop; files in [[file:evidence/DL-experiment/][evidence/DL-experiment/]]) — and
report an honest /negative/: our adaptation of the paper's SDLT and DLEB encodings
([[file:evidence/DL-experiment/sdlt.lp][sdlt.lp]],
[[file:evidence/DL-experiment/dleb.lp][dleb.lp]], transcribed from Listings 1+5+6 and
1+7+8) returned =UNKNOWN= in every 90-second run — unable even to /confirm/ a tour of
3,541 km exists, where naive clingo at least reached 7,592 km. (Those two runs used the
pre-correction distance table; the corrected instance re-runs naive clingo to 6,709 km,
but =clingo-dl= is no longer installed on this machine, so the SDLT/DLEB runs have /not/
been repeated and their numbers are quoted as of 2026-07-18. [badge:stale]) This does
*not* refute the paper; it measures the gap between citing a method and reproducing one.
Known differences: our clingo-dl 1.5 build lacks the =--minimize-variable= option their
tooling uses (we substituted an outer binary search on the bound); our instance is a
/path/-TSP closed by a zero-cost dummy node; and we implemented none of their geometric
(convex-hull) enhancements. Any of these may be decisive — finding out is a conversation
to have with the authors, which seems fitting for a memento. [badge:AI-generated,
verified] (the runs happened as described) [badge:unverified] (any explanation of /why/)
